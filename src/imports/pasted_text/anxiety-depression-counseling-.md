Anxiety Counselling :



1.	What is the main cause of my anxiety?
Anxiety can come from many different places, including stress, past experiences, childhood traumas, relationship patterns, life changes, ongoing pressure, or even genetics. In counselling, we gently explore what may be contributing to your anxiety and how it shows up in your daily life.
2.	Is online anxiety counselling as effective as in-person counselling?
Yes. Research has shown that online anxiety counselling can be as effective as in-person counselling. It offers comfort, flexibility, and easier access to support, especially if anxiety makes it difficult to attend sessions in person. Being in a familiar space may also help some clients feel more relaxed and open during the session. The most important part is having a private space, a stable internet connection, and a counsellor you feel comfortable with.


3.	Should I take medication for anxiety?
Medication can be helpful for some people, but it is not the only option. If you are wondering about medication, it is best to speak with your family doctor or psychiatrist while also exploring counselling support.
4.	Do you use medication?
No. Counsellors do not prescribe medication. Our work focuses on emotional support, understanding your anxiety, building coping tools, and exploring the deeper patterns behind it.
5.	How soon will I feel better?
Many people notice small shifts early on, with deeper change building over time. Everyone’s process is different. Some people feel relief after a few sessions, while others need more time to understand and manage their anxiety. We will move at a pace that feels safe and supportive for you.
6.	Do I have to discuss my past in anxiety counselling?
Not unless it feels helpful or necessary. We can begin with what is happening in your life right now, and if past experiences seem connected to your anxiety, we can explore them gently and at your pace.You’re always in control of what you share.
7.	What if I’ve tried therapy before and it didn’t help my anxiety?
That can feel discouraging, but it does not mean counselling cannot help. Many people find that traditional talk therapy only addresses the mind, but anxiety is not only something we think about; it can also live in the body through tension, restlessness, panic, sleep problems, or feeling constantly on edge. In anxiety counselling, we look at both your thoughts and your nervous system, while using a supportive and trauma-informed approach. Sometimes a different counsellor, pace, or way of working can make the process feel more helpful and meaningful.



8.	What happens if I don’t get anxiety treatment?
Untreated anxiety can sometimes become more intense or start affecting sleep, relationships, work, school, confidence, and daily life. As our nervous system get chronically dysregulated, it can lead to depression as well. Counselling can help you understand your anxiety and find healthier ways to manage it.
9.	How often do I need to attend anxiety counselling sessions?
Many clients begin with weekly sessions. The frequency can be adjusted based on your needs, goals, progress, and availability.
10.	How long does anxiety counselling take?
The length of anxiety counselling can be different for each person. Some people may feel noticeable relief within a few sessions, especially when they are looking for focused support around a specific stressor or situation. Others may benefit from longer-term counselling, especially if anxiety has been present for a long time, feels intense, or is connected to past experiences, relationships, or deeper emotional patterns. Progress is not always linear, and we will work at a pace that feels supportive, realistic, and sustainable for you.
11.	What if I’m not sure anxiety counselling is right for me?
You are welcome to book a free consultation. We can talk briefly about your needs and help you decide whether anxiety counselling feels like a good fit.
12.	Is anxiety counselling covered by insurance?
Many extended health plans cover counselling with a Registered Clinical Counsellor. Coverage depends on your insurance provider, and we provide receipts you can submit for reimbursement. Please check with your insurance provider to confirm your coverage.








Depression Counselling:

1.	What is the main cause of depression?
Depression is a complex mental health issue. It does not usually have one single cause. It can develop from a combination of biology, childhood traumas, personal experiences, emotional stress, burnout, relationship difficulties, family history, and larger life or social pressures. In counselling, we take time to understand the different layers of your depression so the treatment can feel thoughtful, personal, and meaningful.

2.	Does counselling help depression?
Yes. Counselling can help you better understand your emotions, thoughts, relationships, and patterns. It can also support you in finding healthier ways to cope, reconnect with yourself, and slowly move toward meaningful change.
3.	I feel like I’m lazy. Is that depression?
What feels like laziness may actually be a sign that your body and mind are overwhelmed or emotionally shut down. Depression can affect motivation, energy, focus, and the ability to do even simple tasks. For some people, depression is not always obvious from the outside; they may still go to work, care for others, and meet responsibilities while feeling exhausted or disconnected inside.

4.	What is treatment-resistant depression?
Treatment-resistant depression usually means depression that has not improved as expected after trying treatment, such as antidepressants, counselling, or both. It does not mean there is no hope; it may simply mean a different therapeutic approach, more time, or additional support is needed.
5.	What if I don’t have energy to start counselling?
That is completely understandable and welcomed. Depression can make even small steps feel heavy. We can begin slowly, at your pace, and you do not need to come prepared or know exactly what to say. Showing up is more than enough.
6.	Is medication necessary in addition to counselling?
Medication can be helpful for some people, but it is not always necessary. If you are wondering about medication, it is best to speak with your family doctor or psychiatrist while also exploring counselling support.
7.	What if I don’t experience immediate improvement?
Healing from depression can take time. Some people feel relief early on, while others notice progress more gradually. We will work together patiently and adjust the process based on your needs and progress.
8.	How long does it take to start feeling better?
Every journey is unique. Some people may begin to feel a little lighter after a few sessions, while others need more time before they notice changes. Progress may happen slowly, through small shifts in mood, energy, self-understanding, or how you respond to daily life.
9.	How is depression counselling different from just talking to a friend?
Support from friends can be very valuable, but counselling offers a private, professional, and focused space. Your counsellor helps you explore deeper patterns, emotions, and coping strategies without judgment.
10.	Can depression counselling be conducted online?
Yes. Depression counselling can be offered online, allowing you to attend sessions from a private and comfortable space. It can be equally effective as in-person therapy for many individuals, specially when low energy or motivation makes it difficult to come in person.
11.	Can online depression counselling be as effective as in-person counselling?
Yes. Research has shown that online depression counselling can be just as effective as in-person sessions. It offers flexibility, comfort, and easier access to support, while still creating a meaningful therapeutic connection and long-lasting changes.
12.	What happens if I don’t receive treatment for depression?
Without support, depression can sometimes become more intense or begin to affect sleep, relationships, work, school, confidence, and daily routines. Untreated depression can also increase the risk of unhealthy habits such as drug and alcohol addiction. Counselling can help you understand what is happening and begin taking gentle steps toward feeling better.
13.	Is it necessary to discuss my past in depression counselling?
Not always. We can begin with what is happening in your life right now. If past experiences seem connected to your depression, we can explore them gently and only when it feels helpful.
14.	How often do I need to attend depression counselling sessions?
Many clients begin with weekly sessions. The frequency can be adjusted based on your needs, goals, progress, and availability.
15.	How long does depression counselling take?
There is no fixed timeline. Some people come for short-term or focused support during a difficult period, while others continue longer to work through deeper emotional patterns, long-standing depression, or life experiences that may be connected to how they feel today.
16.	What if I’m not sure depression counselling is right for me?
You are welcome to book a free consultation. We can talk briefly about your needs and help you decide whether this service feels like a good fit.

Trauma Counselling:


1.	What is trauma?
Trauma can happen when an experience feels too overwhelming, unsafe, or difficult to process at the time. It may come from one painful event, or from repeated experiences over time that affect your sense of safety, trust, and connection.
2.	Is therapy effective for healing trauma?
Yes, therapy can be very helpful for healing trauma. Healing does not mean forgetting what happened. It means feeling less controlled by the past, understanding your responses, and building more safety, choice, and calm in your life.
3.	Will I have to talk about the details of what happened?
Not unless and until you’re ready. Trauma counselling does not mean you have to share every detail of what happened. We can begin with how trauma is affecting you now and move at a pace that feels safe and manageable for you.
4.	Can talking about trauma make me feel worse?
It is normal to worry about this. Trauma work can bring up difficult feelings, but it should not feel rushed or overwhelming. A trauma-informed approach focuses on safety, grounding, and helping you stay connected to the present while working through painful experiences.
5.	Everyone experiences trauma. Why should I seek therapy?
Many people go through painful experiences, but trauma affects each person differently. If past experiences are still affecting your emotions, relationships, sleep, body, self-worth, or daily life, counselling can help you understand and heal from their impact.
6.	Am I overreacting, or am I really traumatized?
You are not overreacting. Many people minimize their pain or wonder if what happened was “bad enough.” Trauma is not only about the event itself; it is also about how your mind, body, and nervous system were affected by it. It is important to recognize that dismissing our emotions or calling them an “overreaction” can create shame, loneliness, and further mental difficulties.
7.	Is getting triggered easily a sign of trauma?
It can be. Trauma can make the nervous system more sensitive to certain reminders, emotions, tones, situations, or relationship dynamics. Triggers may show up as anxiety, anger, panic, numbness, shutdown, or feeling suddenly unsafe.
8.	Is it too late to heal from old trauma?
No. It’s never too late. Even if something happened many years ago, healing is still possible. Trauma counselling can help you understand old wounds, reduce their emotional weight, and build new ways of feeling safer and more present.
9.	What happens if I don’t address my trauma?
Unaddressed trauma can sometimes continue to affect mood, relationships, sleep, boundaries, self-worth, and how safe you feel in your body. Some people cope by avoiding feelings, staying busy, shutting down, or becoming easily overwhelmed. Counselling can help you gently work through what has been carried for too long.
10.	How do I know I am ready for trauma therapy?
You do not need to feel completely ready. If you are tired of carrying it alone, noticing that the past still affects your present that may be enough to begin slowly.
11.	Can online trauma counselling be as effective as in-person counselling?
Yes. Research has shown online trauma counselling can be as effective as in-person counselling, allowing you to do this deep work from a space where you already feel most secure. matters most is having privacy, a stable internet connection, and a counsellor you feel safe with.
12.	What approaches do you use?
At ChangeMoment Mental Health Center, we use a trauma-informed and evidence-based methods tailored to your needs. We may work with emotions, thoughts, body responses, nervous system regulation, coping patterns, and relationship experiences. The goal is to help you feel safer, more grounded, and more connected to yourself.
13.	What if I tried therapy before and it didn’t help?
That can feel discouraging, but it does not mean therapy cannot help. Trauma counselling depends on many things, including the therapeutic relationship, the pace of the work, and whether the approach feels right for you. Sometimes healing requires a different kind of support—one that focuses not only on talking, but also on safety, the nervous system, and how trauma lives in the body.
14.	How often do I need to attend trauma counselling sessions?
Many clients begin with weekly sessions. The frequency can be adjusted based on your needs, goals, progress, and availability.
15.	How long does trauma counselling take?
The length of therapy can vary depending on the nature of trauma, your personal needs, and the therapeutic approach used. Some people may see significant improvements from focused support, while others continue longer to work through deeper wounds, long-standing patterns, or trauma that has affected many parts of life. Healing from trauma is often gradual, and we will work at a pace that feels safe, realistic, and sustainable for you.

Grief Counselling :

1.	What is grief counselling?
Grief counselling offers a therapeutic space to process loss, understand your emotions, and find ways to live with grief over time. It does not rush you to “move on,” but helps you carry your loss with more care and support and navigate your unique healing journey.
2.	Is it normal to still grieve after a long time?
Yes. Grief has no deadline. Even after months or years, certain memories, dates, places, or life changes can bring strong emotions back.
3.	How do I know if I need professional help for my grief?
You may benefit from grief counselling if your grief feels overwhelming, isolating, or hard to manage alone, or if it is preventing you from function in daily life. It can also be helpful when you need a place to share your grief openly, without feeling that you have to protect your loved ones from your pain.
4.	
5.	Will grief counselling take away the painful emotions?
Grief counselling does not erase the pain of loss, but it can help you understand and move through the emotions in a gentler way. Over time, the pain may feel less heavy and more manageable.
6.	Is it normal to feel angry during grief?
Yes. Anger can be a natural and valid part of grief. You may feel angry at the situation, yourself, others, or even the person you lost. Counselling can help you explore these feelings without judgment.
7.	Will therapy make me forget?
No. Grief counselling is not about forgetting your loved one or your loss. It is about finding a way to remember, honour, and live your life fully while making space for your grief.
8.	Can grief counselling help with non-death-related loss?
Yes. Grief can happen after many kinds of loss, including separation, divorce, infertility, immigration, illness, job loss, life transitions, or losing a sense of identity or safety. We recognize the many ways loss can show up and offer support that reflects your specific needs.
9. I feel relieved after my loved one died. Is this normal?
Yes, this can happen, especially after a long illness, caregiving stress, or a complicated relationship. Relief does not mean you did not love them. Grief can hold many emotions at the same time.
10. How can I support a person who is grieving?
You can support them by listening, being patient, checking in, preparing meals, and not trying to fix or rush their grief. Simple, steady support often means more than finding the perfect words.
11. Do you offer online grief counselling?
Yes. Grief counselling can be offered online, allowing you to receive support from a private and comfortable space. Online sessions can be especially helpful when grief makes it hard to travel, leave home, or fit counselling into your routine.

12. How often do I need to attend grief counselling sessions?

13. How long does grief counselling take?
There is no fixed timeline. Some people come for short-term or focused support after a recent loss, while others continue longer to process complicated grief, major life changes, or deeper emotional pain connected to the loss.

Anger Management Counselling : 


1.	Is anger always a problem?
No. Anger is a normal human emotion, and it can help us recognize that something feels unfair, hurtful, or unsafe. Anger becomes a concern when it feels out of control, happens often, or affects your relationships, work, family, or wellbeing.
2.	How do I know if I have anger issues?
You may benefit from support if your anger feels intense, hard to calm down from, or leads to yelling, blaming, shutting down, breaking trust, or regretting what you said or did. Counselling can help you understand what is underneath the anger and respond in healthier ways.
3.	What is the difference between anger and aggression?
Anger is an emotion. Aggression is a behaviour. You can feel angry without acting aggressively. In counselling, the goal is not to remove anger, but to help you express it in safer, clearer, and more respectful ways.
4.	Do I need to have severe anger issues to benefit from therapy?
No. You do not have to wait until anger becomes severe. Counselling can help even if you simply notice that anger is affecting your communication, stress level, parenting, relationship, or self-esteem.
5.	Will I have to suppress my feelings?
Not at all. Anger management counselling is not about hiding or suppressing your emotions. It is about understanding your anger, recognizing your triggers, and learning how to express your feelings without harming yourself or others.
6.	Why do my anger management skills sometimes not work?
Skills may not work when emotions are already too intense, when your nervous system feels overwhelmed, or when deeper stress, hurt, fear, or trauma has not been addressed. Counselling can help you understand why anger keeps showing up and what support you need beyond quick techniques.
7.	What is the best treatment for anger?
There is no one-size-fits-all treatment. Many people benefit from counselling that includes emotional awareness, communication skills, coping tools, nervous system regulation, and exploring the deeper patterns behind anger. The best approach depends on your needs and what is contributing to your anger.
8.	Will I have to talk about my past in anger management therapy?
Not always. We can begin with what is happening in your life now and how anger is affecting you today. If past experiences seem connected to your anger, we can explore them gently and only when it feels helpful.
9.	Can anger management therapy be done online?
Yes. Anger management counselling can be offered online, allowing you to attend from a private and comfortable space. Online sessions can still support self-awareness, emotional regulation, and healthier communication.
10.	How often do I need to attend anger management counselling sessions?
Many clients begin with weekly sessions. The frequency can be adjusted based on your needs, goals, progress, and availability.
11.	How long does anger management counselling take?
There is no fixed timeline. Some people come for short-term or focused support to learn practical tools, while others continue longer to explore deeper emotional patterns, relationship dynamics, or past experiences connected to anger.

Pregnancy & Postpartum:

1.	What is pregnancy and postpartum counselling?
Pregnancy and postpartum counselling offers emotional support during pregnancy, after birth, or during the transition into parenthood. It can help with anxiety, low mood, identity changes, relationship stress, birth experiences, and the many emotions that can come with this stage of life.
2.	Is it normal to feel emotional during pregnancy or after having a baby?
Absolutely. Pregnancy and postpartum can bring many emotional changes. You may feel joy, fear, sadness, irritability, guilt, or overwhelm. Counselling can help when these feelings feel heavy, confusing, or hard to manage alone.
3.	What is the difference between baby blues and postpartum depression or anxiety?
Baby blues are usually temporary and often improve within the first couple of weeks after birth. Postpartum depression or anxiety may last longer, feel more intense, or affect your sleep, mood, bonding, relationships, or daily functioning.
4.	How do I know if I need professional support?
You may benefit from counselling if you feel overwhelmed, anxious, disconnected, sad, angry, guilty, or unlike yourself. Support can also be helpful if you are having difficulty coping, bonding, sleeping, or adjusting to pregnancy or parenthood.
5.	I feel guilty because I am not enjoying pregnancy or motherhood. Is this normal?
Yes, many people feel this way, even when they love their baby or wanted to become a parent. Pregnancy and parenthood can be emotionally complex. Counselling gives you a space to speak honestly without judgment.
6.	Can counselling help if I feel disconnected from my baby or from myself?
Yes. Feeling disconnected can happen for many reasons, including stress, anxiety, depression, exhaustion, birth experiences, or major life changes. Counselling can help you understand what is happening and gently rebuild connection with yourself and your baby.
7.	Can counselling help after a difficult birth, miscarriage, infertility, or pregnancy loss?
Yes. Pregnancy and postpartum counselling can support many types of reproductive and parenting-related experiences, including difficult birth experiences, pregnancy loss, infertility stress, or changes in your hopes and expectations.
8.	Is counselling only for mothers?
No. Partners and non-birthing parents can also experience emotional distress, anxiety, depression, relationship stress, or identity changes during pregnancy and postpartum. 
9.	Is medication necessary for postpartum depression or anxiety?
Medication can be helpful for some people, but it is not always necessary. Counselling can be an important part of support. If you are wondering about medication, it is best to speak with your family doctor, midwife, nurse practitioner, or psychiatrist.
10.	Do you offer online pregnancy and postpartum counselling?
Yes. Online counselling can be especially helpful during pregnancy or postpartum when leaving home, arranging childcare, or travelling feels difficult. You can attend from a private and comfortable space.
11.	Can online pregnancy and postpartum counselling be as effective as in-person counselling?
Yes, online counselling can be helpful for many clients. It offers flexibility, comfort, and easier access to support while still allowing for a meaningful therapeutic connection.
12.	Can counselling support my relationship during pregnancy or postpartum?
Yes. This stage can bring changes in roles, intimacy, communication, sleep, responsibilities, and emotional needs. Counselling can help you understand these changes and communicate with more care and clarity.
13.	How often do I need to attend pregnancy and postpartum counselling sessions?
Many clients begin with weekly sessions. The frequency can be adjusted based on your needs, goals, progress, and availability.
14.	How long does pregnancy and postpartum counselling take?
There is no fixed timeline. Some people come for short-term or focused support during pregnancy or early postpartum, while others continue longer to work through deeper emotional patterns, relationship changes, birth experiences, or ongoing adjustment to parenthood.

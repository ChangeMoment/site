What Is CVAP?
CVAP stands for the Crime Victim Assistance Program. It is a Government of British Columbia program that may provide financial assistance and benefits to eligible victims of violent crime, immediate family members of injured or deceased victims, and some witnesses. These benefits may include counselling services, medical or dental expenses, prescription drug expenses, protective measures, transportation to counselling, and other supports related to recovery. CVAP is designed to help people cope with the effects of violent crime and support their healing.
How to Get Started With CVAP Counselling
If you have experienced a violent crime in British Columbia and want to access counselling through CVAP, the process usually begins with an application to the Crime Victim Assistance Program.
•	Choose the right CVAP application
There are different application forms for victims, immediate family members, and witnesses. Choose the form that best fits your situation.
•	Complete the application form
The application asks for your personal information, details about the crime, whether a report was made, and what benefits you are requesting, such as counselling.
•	Include counselling as a requested benefit
If you are seeking counselling support, make sure counselling is selected or clearly included in your application.
•	Submit your application to CVAP
Completed applications and attachments can be submitted to CVAP by email, fax, or mail.
•	Ask for help if the form feels overwhelming
A local victim service program can help you complete the application. You can contact VictimLinkBC to find support near you.
•	If time has passed, still check your options
The victim application asks whether the application is being filed within one year of the crime. If not, it asks you to briefly explain why you did not apply sooner.
Who Can Benefit From CVAP Counselling?
•	Victims of violent crime
You may be eligible for support if you experienced physical or psychological injury because of a violent crime in British Columbia.
•	Survivors of abuse or assault
Counselling can support survivors of sexual violence, physical assault, intimate partner violence, or other traumatic violations.
•	Immediate family members
Family members may need support when someone they love has been injured or killed because of a crime.
•	Witnesses impacted by crime
Some witnesses may experience fear, shock, guilt, anxiety, or trauma after seeing or being closely affected by a violent event.
•	People feeling unsafe after trauma
You may feel constantly alert, afraid, disconnected, or unable to relax after what happened.
•	People struggling to return to daily life
Work, relationships, sleep, parenting, school, or basic routines may feel much harder after a traumatic experience.
